How Daylight Commons is measured

Each sign's measured precision and recall with its interval, the data behind it, the side-balance check and the known limits.

How it works Install Privacy How it is measured Catalogue Source

Updated 2026-10-23.

Every sign in Daylight Commons' window either shows how often it was right when people checked by hand, or says "not measured yet". This page lists every sign, the data each was checked on and its licence, how evenly the signs work whichever side a post targets, and what the numbers do not prove.

How to read the numbers

  • Precision is the share of signs Daylight Commons showed that the human annotators of a public research corpus agreed with. It answers "when Daylight Commons shows this, how often is it right?"
  • Recall is the share of the annotated cases that Daylight Commons found. Daylight Commons is built for precision: a wrong sign on an honest reply is the harm it avoids most, so recall is low.
  • Each figure gives the count and a 95% interval (Wilson). A wide interval means few cases were checked.
  • All figures come from a held-out test split (seed radar-eval-2026-10-21). Rules were tuned on a separate split, and the test split was read once, after tuning. Measured 2026-10-23.

One figure per sign

Each sign leads with what its measurement means when you see it. The figure draws the 95% interval as a band and the measured value as a mark; a dashed line is the bar a sign must clear to show. Counts, data and limits sit under "How we know".

The thread report's "extreme"

Precision against Measuring Hate Speech severity labels: 70.3% (903 of 1,285; 67.7% to 72.7%). Recall: 17.3% (903 of 5,224; 16.3% to 18.3%). On HateXplain: precision 60.0% (1,951 of 3,252; 58.3% to 61.7%). Most severe replies do not count as extreme, so the report undercounts them.

Side balance

Political side is never an input to Daylight Commons. To check that the signs work alike whichever side a post targets, the strong language signs were measured on hostile text aimed at groups each side is often hostile to. "Balanced" means the two recall intervals overlap and the larger is at most 1.25 times the smaller.

Documented influence operations

Daylight Commons carries 98 influence operations from 27 attributed countries, first published between 2018 and 2026, and 1,237 domains and accounts their sources named (1,194 domains). A link or account that a report named shows "Named in a published report", with who the sources say ran it, in each source's own words.

What the counts mean. These are operations that investigators found and published, counted by the year of the first report. They are not a measure of how much activity each country runs: platforms look harder in some places and languages than others, and some governments publish takedowns while others never do.

Every operation meets the same bar, whatever the country: at least one primary source that was read (a platform report, a research lab, a court or government document, or reporting that names its sources), an operator type that a source itself states, and a cited source for every field. A test enforces this on the data.

What is not measured

  • Daylight Commons' own rules with no measurement yet: Call for vigilantes, Named conspiracy theory, Leaked AI text, Machine-writing signs, Spam pattern, Hate symbol in name, Media older than the post, Same upload, many accounts, Same words in other threads, Same link or upload in several languages, One person's story, no wider picture, Material passed through other accounts, Fact-checked claim, Look-alike news address, App store elsewhere, Flag of another country, Profile says elsewhere, Active hours fit elsewhere, Replies within a minute, Posts through an automation app, Posts around the clock, Almost only reposts, Very high post rate, New account, Renamed, Sign-up handle, Follows many, few follow back, Low karma for its age, Name borrows a brand. The window says "not measured yet" beside each of them.
  • Same text as other replies, and speaks as local, labelled elsewhere: tested only on cases written alongside the rules (below). No error rate on real replies exists yet.
  • Live threads: no thread from X, YouTube, Reddit, Facebook, Instagram or Bluesky has been labelled by hand yet, so no figure here comes from those sites.
  • French language signs: too few hits in the French corpus to measure. Persuasion cues in French: no corpus Daylight Commons may use.
  • Call for vigilantes: fewer than 20 hits in the test split.
  • Site labels (Community Notes, ads, posting app, X's spam label) and matches against cited tables (outlet owners, state links) are not Daylight Commons' judgement. Their evidence is the site or the cited source.

Known limits

  • Precision. The corpora come from Reddit, Twitter, Gab, YouTube and news comments, several years old. Two of them were sampled to be rich in hate speech (about 61% hostile). On Civil Comments, where 8% of comments are hostile, about a third of strong labels (23 of 68) fell on text the raters called toxic. Real reply threads sit somewhere between. Some Civil "false positives" are quoted or ironic uses that raters scored below 0.5, so 34% is a floor more than a point estimate.
  • Recall. Daylight Commons is a hand-built lexicon and grammar. It finds a small share of hostile text: MHS item recall is under 3% for every family. A thread with no markers is not a clean thread.
  • Weak families. Conspiracy, absolutist and essentialise describe framing, which these corpora do not label. Their figures against hostility labels are context, not error rates.
  • Extreme. The thread report is only as good as "extreme". At 17% recall on MHS, most severe replies do not count as extreme.
  • Synthetic. The duplicate and mismatch figures come from cases written alongside the rules. No person other than the rules' author, and no model, labelled them.
  • PTC is news articles from 48 outlets, mostly 2017 to 2019. Replies are shorter and blunter, and precision there is not measured. It could be higher or lower.
  • PTC's labels are not exhaustive. A true use the annotators did not mark counts as a miss, so precision leans low.
  • A technique describes how a post argues. It does not say the post is false, or anything about who wrote it.

Your own feedback

Each sign in the window has "This was right" and "This was wrong". Your marks stay in your browser. After 10 marks on one kind of sign, the window shows your own count under the measured figure. It never changes the figures on this page. You can save your marks to a file from the options page and choose whether to share it.
