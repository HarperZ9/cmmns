How Radar is measured

Updated 2026-10-21.

Every sign in Radar's window either shows how often it was right when people checked by hand, or says "not measured yet". This page lists every sign, the data each was checked on and its licence, how evenly the signs work whichever side a post targets, and what the numbers do not prove.

How to read the numbers

  • Precision is the share of signs Radar showed that the human annotators of a public research corpus agreed with. It answers "when Radar shows this, how often is it right?"
  • Recall is the share of the annotated cases that Radar found. Radar is built for precision: a wrong sign on an honest reply is the harm it avoids most, so recall is low.
  • Each figure gives the count and a 95% interval (Wilson). A wide interval means few cases were checked.
  • All figures come from a held-out test split (seed radar-eval-2026-10-21). Rules were tuned on a separate split, and the test split was read once, after tuning. Measured 2026-10-21.

The thread report's "extreme"

Precision against Measuring Hate Speech severity labels: 70.3% (896 of 1,275; 67.7% to 72.7%). Recall: 17.2% (896 of 5,224; 16.2% to 18.2%). On HateXplain: precision 60.1% (1,950 of 3,246; 58.4% to 61.7%). Most severe replies do not count as extreme, so the report undercounts them.

Side balance

Political side is never an input to Radar. To check that the signs work alike whichever side a post targets, the strong language signs were measured on hostile text aimed at groups each side is often hostile to. "Balanced" means the two recall intervals overlap and the larger is at most 1.25 times the smaller.

What is not measured

  • Radar's own rules with no measurement yet: Call for vigilantes, Leaked AI text, Machine-writing signs, Spam pattern, Hate symbol in name, Media older than the post, Same upload, many accounts, Same words in other threads, Fact-checked claim, App store elsewhere, Flag of another country, Profile says elsewhere, Active hours fit elsewhere, Replies within a minute, Posts through an automation app, Posts around the clock, Almost only reposts, Very high post rate, New account, Renamed, Sign-up handle, Follows many, few follow back, Low karma for its age. The window says "not measured yet" beside each of them.
  • Same text as other replies, and speaks as local, labelled elsewhere: tested only on cases written alongside the rules (below). No error rate on real replies exists yet.
  • Live threads: no thread from X, YouTube, Reddit, Facebook, Instagram or Bluesky has been labelled by hand yet, so no figure here comes from those sites.
  • French language signs: too few hits in the French corpus to measure. Persuasion cues in French: no corpus Radar may use.
  • Call for vigilantes: fewer than 20 hits in the test split.
  • Site labels (Community Notes, ads, posting app, X's spam label) and matches against cited tables (outlet owners, state links) are not Radar's judgement. Their evidence is the site or the cited source.

Known limits

  • Precision. The corpora come from Reddit, Twitter, Gab, YouTube and news comments, several years old. Two of them were sampled to be rich in hate speech (about 61% hostile). On Civil Comments, where 8% of comments are hostile, about a third of strong labels (23 of 68) fell on text the raters called toxic. Real reply threads sit somewhere between. Some Civil "false positives" are quoted or ironic uses that raters scored below 0.5, so 34% is a floor more than a point estimate.
  • Recall. Radar is a hand-built lexicon and grammar. It finds a small share of hostile text: MHS item recall is under 3% for every family. A thread with no markers is not a clean thread.
  • Weak families. Conspiracy, absolutist and essentialise describe framing, which these corpora do not label. Their figures against hostility labels are context, not error rates.
  • Extreme. The thread report is only as good as "extreme". At 17% recall on MHS, most severe replies do not count as extreme.
  • Synthetic. The duplicate and mismatch figures come from cases written alongside the rules. No person other than the rules' author, and no model, labelled them.
  • PTC is news articles from 48 outlets, mostly 2017 to 2019. Replies are shorter and blunter, and precision there is not measured. It could be higher or lower.
  • PTC's labels are not exhaustive. A true use the annotators did not mark counts as a miss, so precision leans low.
  • A technique describes how a post argues. It does not say the post is false, or anything about who wrote it.

Your own feedback

Each sign in the window has "This was right" and "This was wrong". Your marks stay in your browser. After 10 marks on one kind of sign, the window shows your own count under the measured figure. It never changes the figures on this page. You can save your marks to a file from the options page and choose whether to share it.
