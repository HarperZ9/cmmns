Radar privacy policy

Updated 2026-10-21.

Radar runs entirely in your browser. It has no server, no account, no analytics and no telemetry. Nothing Radar reads or works out is sent to the author or to anyone else.

What Radar reads

Radar reads what the site you are on has already shown or loaded for you: the replies on the page, the account details the site discloses (for example X's "About this account" label), and the post data the site's own app downloaded. It reads nothing the site does not show its viewers, and never reads IP addresses.

Requests Radar makes

Radar sends no data to the author, to analytics services or to any server of its own. It makes these requests, all to the site you are already using, with that site's own session in your browser:

SiteRequestWhen
XThe "About this account" data for reply authors, through the same request X's own app makes, at most one account every 1.5 seconds and slower when X says it is busyAutomatically, unless you switch off "Read account details automatically"
XOne account's profile and About page in a minimised window, so Radar can learn X's request the first time, or when you ask Radar to read accountsThe first time, and when you ask
RedditReddit's public account data (/user/<name>/about.json) for comment authors, one account every 2 secondsAutomatically, unless you switch that setting off

Fact-check matching runs against a table bundled inside Radar, so post text is never sent to a fact-checking service.

The window offers reverse image search links (Google Lens, TinEye, Bing, Yandex) for media. Those open only when you click one, and then that service receives the image address.

Media checks, only when you click. "Check this file" re-requests one image or video file the page already loaded, from the exact address the page used (for X, its own media server), without cookies, so the browser usually answers from its cache. Radar reads the file's Content Credentials and metadata in the tab and keeps the result in memory only. "Take 4 frames" copies frames from the page's own video player in the tab; choosing a search service then copies one frame to your clipboard and opens that service, which receives nothing until you paste the frame there. Credentials stored online (a file that points to a remote manifest) are never fetched.

What Radar keeps, where, and for how long

Everything is kept in chrome.storage.local (Firefox: browser.storage.local) in the browser profile you installed Radar in. It is not synced to other devices.

Post data from the page (views, posting app, media upload times) is held in memory for the tab and is gone when you close it.

Your controls

  • Options page: switch Radar off per site or pause it everywhere, change how long it keeps data, and press "Clear all Radar data" to delete everything above except your settings.
  • History page: clear the history, connections and trails on their own.
  • Save report: writes a JSON file to your computer only when you click it. It holds the thread report, account labels and flags, and leaves reply text out.
  • Save feedback to a file (options page): writes your marks to a JSON file on your computer only when you click it. Radar never sends it. You choose whether to share it, for example by attaching it to an issue on Radar's repository. "Clear feedback" deletes your marks.
  • Removing Radar deletes everything it kept.

What Radar never does

  • Sends anything to the author or a third party.
  • Sells, shares or uses data for advertising.
  • Guesses who runs an account, or anyone's age, gender, ethnicity, religion or politics.
  • Links accounts across sites.

Changes

Changes to this policy are listed in Radar's changelog with the version that made them.

Contact: open an issue on Radar's repository.
