# Governance

## Purpose

Daylight Commons exists to preserve the commons: to let anyone see the evidence behind what they read, and the
machinery acting on what they see. It serves that purpose and its principles, not any individual
agenda, including the author's own. The test for any feature, finding or funding decision is
whether it serves the commons and the principles, not whether it serves a person, a funder or a
faction. The principles themselves can be improved through the same public challenge process as the
catalogue.

Daylight Commons' commitments to its users:

- **Users are never the product.** No data sales, no ad targeting, no behavioural profiles. Data
  stays on the user's device unless they choose to share it, and they can export or delete
  everything in one step. Feedback is asked for, not harvested: the user sees what they send.
- **No models of the crowd for steering it.** Daylight Commons shows people the machinery acting on them and
  never becomes that machinery: no engagement optimisation and no nudging for the project's benefit.
- **Transparency.** Method, catalogue, budget, funders, mistakes and corrections are public. What
  cannot be public, such as a source's identity, is protected, and the policy that protects it is
  public.
- **One standard of evidence for everyone.** Every claim Daylight Commons makes about others, including
  platforms, governments and companies, meets the same standard: documented conduct is stated as
  fact; intent is stated as inference with its evidence, or not at all.

## Permanence

Nobody has the right to take this tool away from the public commons. These commitments make that
structural:

1. **The grants are irrevocable.** Every released version of Daylight Commons is licensed under the GNU Affero
   General Public License v3.0 (code) and Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (data, catalogue,
   schemas and documentation). Both licences are irrevocable for as long as their terms are met.
   No one, the author included, can withdraw them from a version already released.
2. **No CLA, only the DCO.** Contributors keep their copyright and license their work under the
   same terms everyone receives. There is no contributor licence agreement, so no single party, the
   author included, holds the right to relicense contributions closed.
3. **Forks are welcome by design.** Anyone may fork Daylight Commons, change it and run it, under the same
   licence. If this project ever stops serving the commons, a fork is the remedy the licence
   provides, and that remedy is never restricted.
4. **The name.** The name "Daylight Commons" may not be used to mislead people into thinking a fork is this
   project or is endorsed by it. A fork should say plainly that it is a fork and of what. This
   protects users from confusion only; forking, renaming and redistributing are never restricted.
5. **Copies outside any one host.** Every release carries a git bundle of the whole source, and
   the public repository is archived by Software Heritage, so Daylight Commons can be rebuilt without any
   single code host.

## Funding

- **Donations, never influence.** Money buys nothing but the work, for everyone equally. No
  donor, grant-maker or sponsor gets editorial control, roadmap rights, early access, a say in any
  finding or catalogue entry, or exemption from Daylight Commons' evidence.
- **Funders are disclosed.** Funders, amounts, dates and any conditions are published with the
  budget and the spending. A funder can be the subject of a Daylight Commons finding like anyone else.
- **Conditions that conflict with these rules are refused.** Daylight Commons declines any grant or
  donation that asks for influence, secrecy or a change of licence.

## Challenge process

Anyone can challenge a catalogue entry, a definition, a threshold, a badge or a finding.
Organisations named by a badge have the same route.

1. **Open a challenge** as an issue titled "Challenge:" and the entry or badge. Say what is wrong,
   with evidence: a source, a counter-example, or a test the rule fails. Keep other people's private
   data out of the issue; if the evidence needs it, say so and the maintainers will ask for a
   private copy.
2. **Public reasoning.** Every challenge gets a written decision in the issue, accepted or
   rejected, with the reasons and the evidence weighed.
3. **Dated corrections.** An accepted challenge changes the catalogue or the code in a pull request
   that links the challenge, and the correction is recorded with its date in the catalogue's
   changelog or `CHANGELOG.md`. Earlier versions stay visible; corrections are never silent.
4. **Same rule for every side.** A rule that changes for one topic or side changes for all, and
   the symmetry tests must still pass.
5. **No standing needed.** A challenge is judged on its evidence, not on who brings it, and not on
   whether it agrees with the maintainers.

## Decisions

Daylight Commons is currently maintained by one person, who decides by the rules above and writes down the
reason for any decision a contributor disputes. These safeguards are not in place yet and are
planned: a mirror on a second code host, a second administrator for the repository, and a steward
body for the long term. This file will say when each exists.
