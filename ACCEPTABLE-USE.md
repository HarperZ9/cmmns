# Acceptable use policy

**Draft for counsel review. Not legal advice. Not in force: this page awaits a lawyer's review and
is not part of CMMNS' terms until that review is done.**

Drafted 2026-10-28. This policy says what CMMNS is for, the few uses the project does not
support, and what the project does when it hears of misuse. It sits beside the licence and does not
change it (see "This policy and the licence" below).

## What CMMNS is for

CMMNS distills. It takes public material apart into its cleanest measurable parts, keeps
the source of every part, and gives the person using it the evidence to reach their own
understanding. Every reading carries its method, its source, its measured rate where one exists, and
what it does not prove.

You may use CMMNS for any lawful purpose. The project names these uses to make plain that
it supports them in full:

- **Journalism and publishing:** reporting, fact-checking, verification, editing, documentary work,
  and preparing material for publication in any medium.
- **Research and teaching:** academic, scientific, social-science, computational and archival
  research, at any institution or none, and teaching and study at any level.
- **Watchdog and accountability work:** scrutiny of governments, public officials, candidates,
  companies, platforms, campaigns, advertisers, influence operations and anyone else whose public
  conduct is a matter of public interest.
- **Human-rights and legal work:** documenting abuses, preserving evidence, supporting litigation,
  complaints, appeals and investigations, and open-source investigation under the Berkeley Protocol
  or any similar method.
- **Study and creative reference:** studying how a body of public work is made (its techniques,
  devices, palettes, structure and method), keeping study copies, building reference boards, and
  learning from it. `CREATORS-AND-IP.md` covers this in detail.
- **Personal use:** reading your own feeds with more context, keeping your own records, examining
  your own accounts and data, and anything else you do for yourself.
- **Platform and safety work:** trust-and-safety analysis, moderation research, detecting spam and
  coordinated campaigns, and auditing what a platform shows.
- **Building on it:** forking, modifying, embedding and redistributing the code under the AGPL-3.0,
  and the data and documentation under CC BY 4.0.

## How the tool treats people

These are product rules the code keeps, stated so that every user knows what the tool produces:

- **Conduct, never character.** CMMNS shows measured and quoted evidence about anyone's
  public material, each reading with its method, its denominator and a link to the posts behind it.
  It never produces a label about a person's traits, honesty, intent or state of mind, except about
  the user themself or a subject who has consented.
- **Your conclusions are yours.** You weigh the evidence and write your own conclusions. The tool
  stores them as yours, dated and linked to the evidence they rest on, and never writes that layer
  about a person.
- **What the page delivered, and no more.** Capture saves only what a page delivered to your browser
  in the clear. The tool does not decrypt protected media, defeat DRM, bypass a login or get around
  any access control.

## Uses the project does not support

The project does not support the following uses, and the support it offers (below) is not available
for them. Each is specific on purpose: anything outside this list is a permitted use.

1. **Harassment.** Using CMMNS to plan, organise or carry out repeated unwanted contact
   with a person, or a campaign of abuse, threats or intimidation directed at a person.
2. **Stalking.** Using it to track a private person's location, movements, routine or whereabouts.
3. **Doxxing.** Using it to find and publish, without consent, a private person's home address,
   private telephone number or email address, government identification number, or the identity
   behind a pseudonymous account, to expose that person to harassment or harm.
4. **Screening private people for eligibility.** Using records built with CMMNS as a
   factor in deciding a private person's eligibility for employment, housing or tenancy, credit,
   insurance or education. In the United States such use can turn the person compiling the records
   into a consumer reporting agency under the Fair Credit Reporting Act.
5. **Unlawful discrimination.** Using it to treat people unfavourably on the basis of a
   characteristic protected by the law that applies to you.
6. **Circumventing access controls.** Modifying or pairing CMMNS to defeat DRM or
   encryption, enter accounts with credentials that are not yours, evade a site's blocking of your
   access, or solve a CAPTCHA by automation.
7. **Mass surveillance.** Using it for systematic monitoring of a population, or of people because
   of their lawful expression, religion, association, trade-union activity or participation in
   protest, by or on behalf of a government, an employer or any other body with power over them.
8. **People-search products.** Selling or supplying records built with CMMNS about private
   people as a commercial people-search, background-check or data-broker product.

Public figures speaking or acting in a public role, organisations, governments and platforms are
not "private people" for points 3, 4 and 8. Reporting on them, quoting them and measuring their
public conduct is a permitted use.

## What the project does on a misuse report

Report suspected misuse privately to the contact address on the maintainer's GitHub profile
(https://github.com/HarperZ9), with "CMMNS misuse" in the subject. Do not post another
person's private data in a public issue.

The project:

1. Acknowledges the report when the maintainer reads it. CMMNS is maintained by one
   person, so no response time is promised.
2. Reads the evidence offered. The project holds no user data and cannot see what any user does with
   the tool (`DATA-PROTECTION.md`), so it acts on what the report shows.
3. May take any of these steps, at its discretion:
   - decline support, help, review or contributions connected with the misuse;
   - close or lock issues and discussions, and remove content from spaces the project runs;
   - block an account from the project's repository and community spaces under
     `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`;
   - ask a fork that presents itself as CMMNS to stop doing so (`GOVERNANCE.md`, "The
     name");
   - refer the reporter to the platform where the misuse happened, or to the authorities, where a
     person is in danger;
   - change the product or this policy so the same misuse is harder.
4. Records the decision and its reason, keeping the reporter's identity and any private data out of
   the public record.

The project does not monitor users, has no means to switch off a copy someone already has, and will
not add one. Those limits are part of the design (`GOVERNANCE.md`).

## Rights the project keeps

The project keeps the right to decline support, review or contributions for any use, to change this
policy at any time by a dated commit, and to change or remove features in later releases. A change
applies from the date of the commit and is listed in `CHANGELOG.md`.
